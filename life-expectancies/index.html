What the adjustment measures

Unadjusted. We average CDC’s 2010–2015 estimates of life expectancy at birth for each county’s census tracts, weighting by their 2010 Census populations. A county needs estimates for tracts covering at least 80% of its 2010 population. This is a county summary of tract estimates, so it can differ from life expectancy calculated directly from a county’s death records.

Adjusted. We fit a linear regression with one observation per county. The inputs are the natural log of median household income and seven of the eight race and ethnicity shares shown above. The shares sum to 100%, so non-Hispanic White is the reference category. We use the 2011–2015 American Community Survey for both inputs.

Adjusted = unadjusted − model prediction + average unadjusted value

This puts the residual back on a years scale. A higher adjusted value means the county’s estimate exceeded what this model predicted. The model describes associations across counties. It cannot estimate the effect of race or income on any individual’s life expectancy.
